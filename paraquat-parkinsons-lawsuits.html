Paraquat Parkinson’s Lawsuits

By David Meldofsky, California-licensed attorney · Founder, Lawsuit Informer

Last updated: September 5, 2026

The people suing over paraquat are mostly farmers, farmworkers and licensed applicators. They mixed it, sprayed it, or worked in fields where it was used, often for years. Then, sometimes decades later, a hand started to shake and a doctor said Parkinson’s disease.

Their lawsuits say the companies that made paraquat knew it could damage the brain and never put that on the label. This page explains what the claims say, who is bringing them, and what records tend to matter. It will not tell you whether you have a case. That depends on your own facts, and it is what a free case review is for.

Important:

This page is general legal education about paraquat litigation. It is not medical or legal advice.

Key Takeaways:
  • These cases claim that long-term paraquat exposure caused Parkinson’s disease.
  • Most people bringing them worked with the herbicide on the job, year after year.
  • What usually matters is your work history, which product you used, and when you were diagnosed.
  • The science linking paraquat to Parkinson’s is contested, and that fight is the heart of the litigation.
Where this stands now:

The federal cases are largely on hold while a settlement the two sides signed in April 2025 is finalized. It has not paid anyone yet. A Supreme Court ruling in June 2026 in the Roundup cases has also raised a question about the main legal theory here. Both are still moving, and we track them on Paraquat Lawsuit Updates.

What Paraquat Is

Paraquat is a weed killer. It is one of the strongest ever sold, and it is so poisonous that a single sip can kill an adult. There is no antidote. It reached the US market in 1964, and farms have used it since to clear fields before planting and to dry crops down before harvest.

Because it is so toxic, only trained and licensed people are allowed to apply it. You cannot buy it at a garden store. For more on the chemical itself and where it is banned, see What Is Paraquat?

What These Lawsuits Say

The core claim is short. Paraquat damages the brain cells that make dopamine. Losing those cells is what causes Parkinson’s disease. The companies that sold paraquat knew about the danger and never warned the people spraying it.

From there the arguments branch out. Some claim the product was dangerous by design. Some claim the companies hid what their own research showed. All of them turn on the same underlying question, which is whether paraquat can cause Parkinson’s at all.

Why Parkinson’s Disease Is at the Center

Parkinson’s is the one diagnosis these cases are built around. It comes on slowly. A tremor, then stiffness, then trouble with balance and walking. There is no cure.

The slow onset is also what makes these cases hard. Someone may stop spraying in 1995 and get diagnosed in 2020. Whether the research ties those two things together is covered on Does Paraquat Cause Parkinson’s Disease?, and the wider category sits on Neurological Conditions Linked to Lawsuits.

Who Is Bringing These Claims

Mostly people who worked with the stuff. Farmers, farmhands, licensed applicators, groundskeepers, and crop dusters. People who mixed it in a tank, walked behind a sprayer, or cleaned the equipment afterward.

Some claims come from people who never touched it. If you lived next to sprayed fields, or washed the clothes of someone who did, you may have been exposed without knowing. What tends to matter is not one bad day but years of contact.

Health Concerns Discussed in These Cases

  • Parkinson’s disease
  • Tremor, stiffness, and slowed movement
  • Trouble with balance and walking
  • Other nerve and brain injury claims

How People Were Exposed

  • Mixing or loading the concentrate into a tank
  • Spraying fields, by hand or from a tractor
  • Clearing a clogged nozzle or line, or cleaning out the sprayer afterward
  • Spray drifting on the wind from a field next door
  • Working in fields shortly after they were treated
  • Well water drawn near ground where it was used
  • Contact through a family member’s work clothes

What the Claims Are Built On

  • Failure to warn about the risk to the brain
  • Defective design of the product itself
  • Negligence in how it was researched, sold and labeled
  • Breach of the basic promise that a product sold is fit to use
  • Deceptive marketing, under state consumer protection laws
  • Hiding what the companies knew internally

A husband or wife can bring a claim of their own alongside it, for the loss of the life the couple had together. It is part of the same case rather than a separate lawsuit.

Why These Cases Are Hard to Prove

Two gaps make paraquat cases difficult. The first is time. Decades can pass between the last spray and the first symptom, and memories and records fade in between.

The second is the science. Many studies have found that people exposed to paraquat get Parkinson’s more often than people who were not. The manufacturers argue those studies rely on what people remember rather than on measured exposure. In 2024 a judge agreed with them far enough to exclude the plaintiffs’ main expert on that question, which threw out the first four test cases.

People looking at this often compare it with Roundup Cancer Lawsuits. Both involve weed killers, but the illness and the evidence are different.

What Records Matter

Most of the work in one of these cases is proving you were around paraquat, and for how long. There is a form that shows exactly how much detail that takes. Everyone with a case in the federal litigation fills out a questionnaire, under oath, and it asks for far more than people expect.

For each stretch of time you used paraquat it wants the dates, your job then, how you applied it, how many acres you covered, roughly how many gallons, how many days a year, and who sold it to you. Then the product name, its strength, and what you mixed it with, which is where knowing which product it was starts to matter. It asks whether the container had a label, whether you read it, and whether you followed it. It asks which protective gear you wore, item by item, down a list of ten.

On the medical side it asks for every doctor you saw in the ten years before your symptoms started, and every neurologist who has ever treated you. It asks about relatives with Parkinson’s, about head injuries, about welding work, and about genetic testing. Those questions are there because the other side will argue something besides paraquat caused your illness, and each of them is a possible other explanation. It also wants photographs and receipts for the equipment you sprayed with, down to the tractor, the tank and the nozzles.

  • Where you worked and lived, with dates
  • An applicator license and its number, if you held one
  • Receipts, invoices or spray logs showing paraquat purchases
  • The product name and strength, if you can find or recall it
  • Photographs of the farm and the spray equipment
  • Your Parkinson’s diagnosis and neurology records
  • Coworkers or family who remember the spraying

Missing an old receipt is normal and not the end of a claim. A lawyer can work with what you have and with what people remember. The questionnaire itself is different: it is mandatory, and more than a hundred cases were dismissed from the federal litigation in June 2025 because it was never returned.
