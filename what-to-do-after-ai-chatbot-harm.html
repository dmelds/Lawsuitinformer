AI Chatbot Harm: What to Do in the First Days

By David Meldofsky, California-licensed attorney · Founder, Lawsuit Informer

Last updated: September 7, 2026

Something happened to someone you love, and a chatbot was part of it. Maybe you found the conversations afterward. Maybe your teenager told you. Whatever brought you here, the record of what was said sits inside an account that can be emptied in a few taps, and parts of it disappear on a clock. This page is about what to keep and what to do in the first few days.

  • 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988. Available 24/7, free, confidential.
  • Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 to connect with a volunteer crisis counselor.

Key Takeaways:
  • The conversation itself is usually the most important record, and deleting it is permanent.
  • Archiving keeps a conversation. Deleting removes it, and support cannot bring it back.
  • An export takes days to arrive, so requesting one early is better than requesting one later.
  • Whether any of it supports a claim depends on the facts, which is a question for a lawyer.

Save the conversation before anything else

The conversation is the only complete record of what was actually said. Screenshots catch pieces of it. Memory blurs, and it blurs faster under stress. What sits in the account is the whole thing, in order, with dates attached.

It is also the part most easily lost, and rarely on purpose. Accounts get tidied up. Phones get handed down or traded in. Someone deletes a thread because reading it hurts. Once a conversation is deleted there is nothing left to recover, and no way to reconstruct it later.

So before anything else: do not delete it, and ask anyone else with access to the account not to delete it either.

  • Deleting is permanent. OpenAI says a deleted conversation leaves the account straight away and is removed from its systems within 30 days. After that it cannot be recovered through the app, through support, or anywhere else.
  • Archiving keeps it. An archived conversation stays in the account and moves out of the main list. If someone wants it out of sight, that is the option that does not lose it.
  • Ask for a copy early. An account holder can request an export of the account data through settings or the privacy portal. With ChatGPT it can take up to seven days to arrive, and the download link expires about a day later. An export will not bring back anything already deleted.
  • Memory is stored separately. If the memory feature was switched on, ChatGPT saved details about the person across conversations. Those saved memories sit apart from the chat history, so they may still exist even where conversations do not.

Character.AI, Replika, Gemini, Copilot and the others each handle deletion and exports their own way. Their help pages are the place to check the specifics. The general approach is the same wherever the conversations live: change nothing, delete nothing, and request an export while you work out what matters.

Leave the account and the device as they are

Accounts and phones get cleaned up in the weeks after something terrible happens. It is a normal impulse, and it is worth resisting for a while.

  • Keep the account open. Closing it can take the conversations, the saved memories and the billing history all at once.
  • Do not reset the phone or tablet. A factory reset or a handover to a family member can wipe app data that exists nowhere else.
  • Keep the device charged and in the house. A device that sits dead in a drawer for months can be harder to get into later.
  • Photograph the screen as a backup. If an export is slow or the login does not work, screenshots or photos of the screen preserve something rather than nothing.
  • Write down the passwords you have. Access is often the thing that gets lost first.

What else is worth keeping

The conversation explains what was said. Everything below helps explain the surrounding circumstances, which is often what a lawyer asks about first.

  • Billing and subscription records. A card statement or receipt usually shows when a paid plan started, which anchors the rest of the timeline.
  • Phone and cloud backups. An older backup may hold app data that the current device no longer has.
  • Messages to friends and family. Texts and social messages from the same period often describe what someone was going through in their own words.
  • School and work records. Attendance, grades, write-ups and performance notes can show when things changed.
  • Health and counseling records. Appointments, referrals and notes from that period are part of the picture, and you can request copies from each provider.
  • People who noticed. A teacher, a coach, a sibling or a friend may remember dates and changes that nobody wrote down.

Write the timeline down while it is fresh

Memory blurs quickly, and it blurs faster under grief and stress. A plain written timeline is one of the more useful things a family can put together in the first week, and it costs nothing.

Keep it factual. When did the person start using the app, as best anyone can tell? What changed, and roughly when? Who noticed what, and on what date? Where a date is a guess, write that it is a guess. A short honest timeline is worth more than a long confident one that turns out to be wrong.

Does any of this mean there is a case?

Whether a particular situation supports a claim depends on the specific facts. That is something to talk through with a lawyer rather than settle from an article, and nothing on this page can answer it for you.

What saving the records does is keep the question open. Families who preserved the conversations were able to find out. Families who lost them often could not, whatever the underlying facts were. Common Questions

How long do I have before a deleted ChatGPT conversation is gone?

OpenAI says a deleted conversation leaves the account straight away and is removed from its systems within 30 days. After that it cannot be recovered through the app or by asking support. If there is any chance the conversation matters, the safest step is to leave it where it is.

Is archiving the same as deleting?

No. Archiving keeps the conversation in the account and moves it out of the main list. If someone wants a conversation out of sight without losing it, archiving is the option that keeps it.

Can I get a copy of everything in the account?

An account holder can request an export of the account data through settings or the platform's privacy portal. With ChatGPT the file can take up to seven days to arrive and the download link expires about a day later. An export will not bring back anything that was already deleted.

Should I close the account?

Closing an account can remove the conversations, the saved memories and the billing history all at once. If you are unsure whether any of it will matter later, keeping the account open costs nothing and keeps your options open.

What if the conversations were on a different app?

Character.AI, Replika, Gemini, Copilot and the rest each handle deletion and exports their own way, and their help pages are the place to check. The general approach holds everywhere: change nothing, delete nothing, and ask for an export before anyone decides what matters.

Does saving all of this mean there is a case?

That depends on the specific facts, and it is a question for a lawyer rather than a web page. Saving the records simply keeps the option available while you find out. A case review through Lawsuit Center is free and carries no obligation.
