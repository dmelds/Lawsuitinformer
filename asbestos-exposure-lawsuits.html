Asbestos Exposure Lawsuits

By David Meldofsky, California-licensed attorney · Founder, Lawsuit Informer

Last updated: September 3, 2026

If you or someone in your family was diagnosed with mesothelioma, asbestos-related lung cancer, or asbestosis, the exposure behind it was probably decades ago. Mesothelioma usually appears 20 to 50 years after a person breathes in asbestos. A job you left in 1978 can be the reason you are sick now, and that old exposure can still support a claim today. This page explains how these cases work and what to do first.

Can You Sue for Asbestos Exposure?

If you were diagnosed with an asbestos-related illness, you may be able to bring a claim against the companies that made, sold, or installed the asbestos you breathed. Whether that holds true in your case depends on your diagnosis, on where and when you were exposed, and on which companies can still be traced to it. Those are facts a lawyer has to look at.

Some people were exposed on the job. Others were exposed in the Navy or aboard ship. Some were exposed at home, from fibers a husband or father carried in on his work clothes.

There can be more than one source of money. Some comes from lawsuits against companies still in business. Some comes from trusts that bankrupt companies were required to set up. Learn more in our Asbestos Trust Funds and Claims guide. For how a case moves along, see How Lawsuits Work, What Evidence Helps a Lawsuit?, and What Happens After You Contact a Lawyer?.

What Is Asbestos Exposure?

Asbestos is a mineral that was packed into insulation, building materials, and industrial equipment for most of the last century because it stands up to heat. It sits harmless as long as nothing disturbs it. Cutting it, tearing it out, or sweeping it up throws off fibers too small to see, and those fibers get breathed in.

The fibers lodge in the lungs and the tissue around them, and the body has no way to clear them out. They stay for the rest of a person's life. Asbestos causes mesothelioma, a cancer of the lining of the chest or the abdomen. It causes asbestosis, which scars the lungs. It also raises the risk of lung cancer.

Who May Qualify for an Asbestos Exposure Lawsuit?

A claim starts with a diagnosis: mesothelioma, asbestos-related lung cancer, or asbestosis. From there the work is tracing where the asbestos came from. The question is when you were exposed, at which job or building, and whose product it was.

What matters most:

  • A diagnosis tied to asbestos
  • A work history that put you around asbestos
  • Records showing how long it went on
  • Evidence tying the exposure to a named employer, manufacturer, contractor, or site

Who Is at Risk for Asbestos Exposure?

Some trades handled asbestos every working day for years.

  • Construction and demolition workers
  • Shipyard and naval workers
  • Industrial and factory workers
  • Pipefitters, boilermakers, and insulation workers
  • Automotive and brake repair workers
  • Family members exposed through secondhand contact

What Evidence May Help Support a Claim?

These cases are built by rebuilding a work history from decades back. Start with the employers. Then the buildings, plants, and ships. Then the products you handled and the men who worked beside you.

What helps:

  • Medical records and pathology reports
  • Employment history and union records
  • Military service records
  • Coworker statements or witness accounts
  • Product identification and work-site history
  • Documentation showing repeated exposure over time

For a closer look at which records carry weight, read What Records Help Support an Asbestos Claim?. Lawyers do this reconstruction all the time, and How Do Lawyers Prove Asbestos Exposure From Decades Ago? explains how. For broader guidance, see What Evidence Helps a Lawsuit?.

Who May Be Responsible for Asbestos Exposure?

The companies on the other side are usually the ones that made the asbestos products, sold them, or ran the site where you worked. Most cases name several at once. Industrial work put people near dozens of different products, across many job sites, over many years.

These are product liability claims, and many of them are handled as mass torts.

Where you were exposed shapes the case. California, for example, lets a seriously ill person get a trial date faster than the usual wait. If your exposure was in California, you can also review California asbestos and mesothelioma lawyers on Lawsuit Center.

Types of Asbestos Claims

What form a claim takes depends on who brings it and who is being sued. Some cases center on one manufacturer's equipment, such as Goulds Pumps and mesothelioma.

  • Personal injury claims, brought by the person who is sick
  • Wrongful death claims brought by surviving family members
  • Trust fund claims involving companies that entered bankruptcy and established asbestos trusts

The first thing most people want to know is what a case is worth. The honest answer is that it turns on the diagnosis, the exposure, and the companies involved, so any single number would mislead you. Our guide to asbestos settlement amounts explains how claims get valued and what moves the range.

How Long Do People Have to File an Asbestos Claim?

Deadlines vary by state and by the kind of claim. In asbestos cases the clock usually starts running at diagnosis, or at the point a doctor connected the illness to asbestos, rather than at the exposure itself.

Because that clock is already running, most people start looking into their options soon after the diagnosis comes in.

Worried it is already too late? Read Is It Too Late to File an Asbestos Claim After Symptoms Appear Years Later?. For broader timing guidance, see Statute of Limitations Basics and How Long Do Lawsuits Take?.

What To Do After an Asbestos-Related Diagnosis

Start with what you can write down. Gather the medical records. List every job, every plant, every ship. Note the products you remember handling and the men who worked next to you. That list is what a lawyer needs in order to tell you whether there is a case.

How long after asbestos exposure do symptoms appear?

Mesothelioma usually appears 20 to 50 years after a person breathes in asbestos. That long gap is why a diagnosis so rarely brings an old job to mind right away. Learn more →

Who may qualify for an asbestos lawsuit?

A claim starts with a diagnosis of mesothelioma, asbestos-related lung cancer, or asbestosis, alongside a history of exposure at work, in the military, or at home. What matters most is the diagnosis, the work history, and evidence tying the exposure to a named employer, manufacturer, or work site. Whether that fits your situation depends on your own facts. What types of asbestos claims may exist?

Claims fall into three groups. A personal injury claim is brought by the person who is sick. A wrongful death claim is brought by surviving family. A trust fund claim goes to a fund that a bankrupt company was required to set up. Learn more →

What symptoms may be linked to asbestos exposure?

Shortness of breath, a cough that will not clear, chest pain, and fatigue are the common ones. They surface decades after the exposure, which makes them easy to put down to age or smoking. Learn more →

Where did asbestos exposure commonly happen?

Most exposure happened in shipyards, on construction sites, and inside power plants, refineries, and factories. The trades hit hardest were pipefitters, boilermakers, insulation workers, Navy personnel, and automotive repair workers. Learn more →
