TRACKED TOPICS

Each topic runs as a Google News search over the window plus one over the last 24 hours, up to 20 topics. A story counts for a topic when its headline or summary names it. Give the other names the press uses after an equals sign, separated by commas: talc lawsuit = talcum powder, baby powder. The stock topics already carry their names, so asbestos catches mesothelioma and PFAS catches forever chemicals. AI topics show in the AI Wire and Intelligencer topics in INTELLIGENCER. NEWS WIRE shows all of them.

FIRMS TO WATCH (ONE PER LINE)

Each firm runs a Google News search for its exact name: verdicts, filings, appointments, press.

WHERE TO LOOK
OUTLETS SEARCHED FOR YOUR TOPICS (ONE DOMAIN PER LINE)

Each outlet runs Google News searches limited to that site, and requires a topic term in the headline. Leave blank to turn this off.

NEWS SITE FEEDS (ONE PER LINE)

RSS feeds that run unfiltered, so everything they publish in the window arrives.

FIRM FEEDS (ONE PER LINE)

A blog page URL works if the page lists its feed.

AI SEARCHES (ONE PER LINE)

Each line runs as its own Google News search for the AI Wire and tags whatever it returns. Quotes force a word to appear, and intitle: forces it into the headline. Google News ignores allintitle:.

AI FEEDS (ONE PER LINE)

Publications that cover AI in law practice. These run unfiltered and show in the AI Wire. A post that covers one of your topics shows in NEWS WIRE too.

WHAT TO SUPPRESS
TRUSTED SOURCES (ONE PER LINE)

A topic search keeps an item when its headline or summary carries the topic. These publishers are exempt, so a Reuters story headlined "forever chemicals" still counts as PFAS. A publisher name as shown on items, or a domain.

BLOCKED SOURCES (ONE PER LINE)

A publisher name as shown on items, or a domain. Applies to every tab.

BLOCKED WORDS (ONE PER LINE)

A story whose headline or summary uses one of these words or phrases is hidden from every tab. Plurals and -ed/-ing forms count too, so "copyright" also hides "copyrighted". Join words with + to hide a story only when it uses all of them: "police + AI" hides police AI stories and leaves other police news. A slash lists choices for one slot: "stock/share + jump/fall" hides "Bayer shares fall" and "Bayer stock jumps".
